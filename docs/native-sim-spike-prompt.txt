Decision rule (user, 2026-11-10; ratios only, so it holds on any later tree). Measure four technology ratios on Deck Firefox, each from runs on one tree in one session, per slice and for both slices together:
- K, language gain: GDScript time for the slice's work divided by Rust time for the same work with the same algorithm, both as WASM. Rust time is the native call minus the pass-through call. GDScript's time includes its own data access, which has no exact counterpart, so K is a range: the whole GDScript slice over the Rust time (upper end), and the same with the packing time subtracted from the GDScript side (lower end). The plan's "Ratios" fixes the arithmetic.
- H, hand-off: packing plus the pass-through call plus applying the outputs, per tick, reported per entity (microseconds and bytes) and as a fraction of the GDScript slice time.
- E, effective gain: GDScript slice time divided by the whole replacement path (packing + call + native work + conversion back + applying + any synchronisation). This is the quantity the old 5x line meant.
- T, template tax: unchanged GDScript whole-tick time on the extension-enabled template divided by the same on today's template, minus 1.
Read two game numbers at decision time, from the tree as it then is, not from this spike's base:
- s: the share of the tick that the candidate slices cover (`sh tools/run_profile.sh`; 2026-11-10: 40.7 % for the two slices).
- Q, the need: the worst simulation row's p95 on Deck Firefox divided by its 10 ms budget (2026-11-08: hordes300, 14.54 / 10 = 1.45; re-measured in this run by the W1 baseline).
Project two paths (estimates, always labelled as such; the gain G is also roughly "how many times more units fit in a tick"):
- Hot loops: G_inc = 1 / ((1 + T) x (1 - s) + s / E). Its ceiling is 1 / ((1 + T) x (1 - s)) however fast the native code is.
- Whole port: G_port = 1 / (1 / K + p), where p is the presentation hand-off as a fraction of today's tick (from the snapshot probe and the per-entity H). Today's `snapshot()` is outside the timed tick, so give G_port twice: with the hand-off charged in full, and with only its cost beyond today's snapshot. K from two slices standing in for the whole simulation is the weakest assumption in this rule; use the lower end of the K range, show the upper end and the spread between the two slices, and say so next to the number.
Decide by comparing with Q times the headroom factor 1.5 (the simulation keeps growing with avoidance, fog and bots, and a toolchain that only just passes is not worth its permanent cost):
- G_inc reaches it for a share s that real slices can cover: pursue incremental extraction.
- Only G_port reaches it: hot loops are a dead end and the choice is whole port or nothing; the next step is then the golden scenarios, not more Rust.
- Neither reaches it: no performance case. The portability case is reported separately and does not rescue it.
Two floors that follow from the arithmetic rather than from taste: a slice is only a net gain if s x (1 - 1/E) is larger than T x (1 - s), and only counts if the measured gain is larger than the A/A spread. Also report the measured whole-tick p95 before and after with both slices native in the two shipped-style configurations; a measured number outranks the projection.
